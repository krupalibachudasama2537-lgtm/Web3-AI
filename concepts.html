Web3 Core Concepts

Side-by-side comparisons of the fundamental ideas powering the decentralised web

🌐 Web2 vs Web3

Web2

  • Data stored on company servers (Google, Meta, Amazon)
  • Accounts owned by platforms — can be banned or deleted
  • Revenue captured by intermediaries
  • Permissioned: companies decide who can build
  • Identity = email + password (company-held)
VS

Web3

  • Data stored on-chain or on decentralised storage (IPFS)
  • Wallet-based identity — only you hold your private key
  • Value flows directly between participants
  • Permissionless: anyone can build or use without approval
  • Identity = cryptographic key pair you control

⚡ Ethereum vs Bitcoin

Bitcoin

  • Designed as peer-to-peer digital cash
  • Simple scripting language, limited programmability
  • Proof of Work consensus (SHA-256 mining)
  • ~10 minute block times, 7 TPS
  • Primary use: store of value, payments
VS

Ethereum

  • Programmable blockchain — "world computer"
  • Turing-complete EVM, runs any smart contract
  • Proof of Stake consensus (The Merge, 2022)
  • ~12 second block times, ~15 TPS (+ L2 scaling)
  • Primary use: DeFi, NFTs, DAOs, smart contracts

🔑 Public Key vs Private Key

Public Key (Address)

  • Derived mathematically from your private key
  • Share it freely — it's your blockchain "username"
  • Used to receive funds and verify signatures
  • Example: 0x742d…3A7B (40 hex characters)
  • Losing it is not a problem — it's public
VS

Private Key

  • A random 256-bit number — your secret password
  • Never share it — whoever has it owns your assets
  • Used to sign transactions and prove ownership
  • Example: 64 random hex characters (256 bits)
  • Losing it means permanent loss of funds

🗄️ Blockchain vs Traditional Database

Traditional Database

  • Controlled by a single company or admin
  • Records can be edited or deleted at any time
  • Fast reads/writes — optimised for performance
  • Trust the company to be honest and secure
  • Single point of failure
VS

Blockchain

  • Maintained by thousands of independent nodes
  • Immutable — past records cannot be changed
  • Slower — every node must verify every transaction
  • Trustless — math and cryptography replace trust
  • No single point of failure — censorship-resistant
